## The Geography of Living Alone

Single-person households have become increasingly common across America, but their distribution isn't random. This analysis maps the "loneliness gradient"—the geographic patterns of social isolation as measured through household composition and community characteristics.

[View full analysis →](/census-monkey-typewriter/analyses/loneliness-gradient.html)

### Key Findings

- Geographic clustering of single-person households
- Urban-rural differences in living alone patterns
- Relationship between household composition and community social capital

### Data Sources

- US Census Bureau American Community Survey
- Household composition data
- Community characteristics and social infrastructure indicators

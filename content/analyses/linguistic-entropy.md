This analysis explores whether counties with higher linguistic diversity exhibit greater economic resilience and innovation, using Shannon entropy of language distributions to quantify diversity patterns.

## Research Question

Can linguistic heterogeneity serve as a predictor of economic dynamism? The hypothesis suggests that areas with diverse language communities may demonstrate enhanced economic performance through cultural bridging, diverse networks, and varied skill sets.

## Key Findings

- Counties with higher linguistic entropy show statistical correlation with economic indicators
- Border counties and established ethnic enclaves demonstrate distinct patterns
- Significant challenges remain in establishing causal relationships versus selection effects
- Geographic clustering reveals "linguistic archipelagos" of high-diversity counties

## Methodological Innovation

The analysis employs Shannon entropy calculations to create a linguistic diversity index:
- Measures distribution evenness across language categories
- Controls for immigrant share and educational attainment
- Uses instrumental variables approach with refugee resettlement patterns
- Includes robustness checks with alternative entropy measures

**Data Sources:** American Community Survey Table B16001 (Languages Spoken at Home), B19013 (Median Income), B05002 (Nativity)  
**Geographic Level:** Counties (2,904 with sufficient population)  
**Methods:** Shannon entropy calculation, instrumental variables regression, spatial analysis
